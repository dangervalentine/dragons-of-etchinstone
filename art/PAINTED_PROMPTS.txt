PAINTED CREATURE PROMPTS (feature 008), iteration 5
====================================================

tools/paint_art.py reads this file and sends SUBJECT + EMANATION + STYLE as one prompt.
Run `python3 tools/paint_art.py prompt <name>` to see exactly what is sent. STYLE is the same for
every creature, EMANATION is filled in the same way for every creature, and every
creature is steered by the same SHARED STYLE REFERENCES. Only the subjects differ.

Each image starts from the creature's old silhouette in flat mid-grey (the START), at
denoise 0.88: the silhouette fixes the pose and outline, and the prompt and references
paint everything inside it.

Write how you picture each character under YOUR VISION, or edit the SUBJECT directly.

Lessons so far:
- Background, shadow and signature are written as what the picture IS ("empty all
  around and beneath it"). FLUX ignores negatives, and naming a thing, even as "no
  tail", tends to draw it. Ground shadows still appear; the cut-out step removes them.
- Tails come from three places: the prompt (only a creature that has one names one),
  the starting silhouette, and the style references. Since iteration 4 only the prompt
  is kept clean (see the tails lesson below).
- Starting from noise gives soft, rendered, semi-3D images. Starting from the flat grey
  silhouette gives flat ones.
- Per-creature references pulled each creature toward its own earlier look, so the set
  didn't match. One shared set of references does.
- Redux above 0.05 copies the references' colours and designs. Keep it at 0.05.
- Colour words in the subjects gave every creature a different palette. The palette is
  now in STYLE, and subjects describe shapes and materials, not colours.
- Without the ferocity sentence, creatures came out cute (iteration 2's lion).
- The emanation is followed reliably at one or two places, rarely at four.
- Iteration 3 (PC, Q8, seeds 81-84) was judged against the user's picks: it rendered
  more fur and gradient than the good ones. The target is the seven GOOD images below:
  flat fills, few big shapes, mean faces, side view, the Ether at the eyes and one place.
- Colour matching across the set is deferred: colours can be adjusted later, or the
  creatures remade from scratch in Illustrator. Don't chase the palette in the prompt.
- Eyes come out yellow or red unless the emanation names them glowing in the Ether's
  colour, so the eyes are now their own sentence in the EMANATION.
- Tails: never prompted, but not fought either. One that appears is removed later.
  Long shapes low behind the legs in a START (the goblin's blade) get painted as tails.
- The ape puts its Ether in its mouth whatever the prompt names.
- An all-grey reference plus the grey START keeps fur grey (the ape-61 anchor, dropped).
- Iteration 3 painted fur as texture, so STYLE now says fur, hair and cloth are smooth
  solid shapes.
- Iteration 3's four creatures are set aside; iteration 4 tries eight new ones, 8
  seeds each, to see how the style holds across the set.
- Iteration 4 (seeds 91-98, stopped after 30 images): seven references at 0.05 each
  copied their designs onto new creatures. The crane and treant grew lion tails, the
  crane a mane, the treant a goblin's body and loincloth, and "wolfish muzzle" plus the
  lions turned the panda into a wolf. References made from a handful of creatures and
  then used for the rest spread that handful's look.
- So iteration 5 paints every enemy from its own silhouette with no references, as a
  clean baseline; references are picked later from the whole set.
- A light direction ("one light falls from the upper left") brought soft shading, low
  tones and highlights (crane 94). STYLE now asks for matte flat colour with at most
  one hard shade tone.
- Faint signatures appear in a corner of some images; the cut-out step crops them.
- Vector test (after iteration 5, tag "vector"): STYLE opened with "A flat 2D game art
  drawing in the style of a modern indie deckbuilder card game", which may pull toward
  card art, mostly painted and shaded. It now opens with the flat-colour vocabulary of
  the SDXL LoRA "Flat 2d Style Cutout" (civitai 284812; SDXL, so it can't load on
  FLUX): flat vector illustration, minimalistic graphic design, flat colour, clean
  lines. Its absence words ("no face", "no background") are left out, per the first
  lesson. Four creatures rerun on iteration 5's seeds 101-104 for a side-by-side.
  Result: almost identical to iteration 5, image for image; the vector wording kept
  (no worse) but made at most a slightly flatter body here and there. On the same seed
  and START, the opening words of STYLE barely move the result, so wording is not the
  lever for flatness. Next levers: a FLUX flat-colour LoRA, or flattening in post.
- Flattening in post (proof of concept from the Mac session, flatten.py: mean-shift and
  bilateral smoothing, k-means to 6 body tones with the saturated Ether masked out and
  quantized on its own, a dark outline), run on 43 iteration 5 picks: bodies, robes and
  fur do turn into flat shapes, and strong, saturated Ether (violet breath, fire, the
  goblin's ice) keeps its colour. It fails on: pale glows (the brute's cyan eyes and
  frost turn white; S>80 misses them), thin detached Ether (the gnome's sparks and the
  crane's wisps get the dark outline drawn round them and turn black), large smooth
  bodies (6 tones break a gradient into camouflage blobs), ground shadows (left as pale
  outlined patches) and signatures (left as outlined black specks). So the prompt still
  carries the flat look, and post-processing tidies it once the background is removed.
- flatten.py v2 (Mac, rembg cut-out, Ether found by its hue): background, shadows and
  signatures are gone; pale cyan eyes and frost, the gnome's sparks and detached wisps
  keep their colour; the outline runs round the body only. Still wrong: a water hue
  window catches slate-blue cloth (cyan stripes on crane 107's robe); small warm eyes
  and pale-yellow lightning drop out (the lion's and wizard's red eyes, boar 106's and
  antler 104's sparks); big smooth bodies still break into blobs (the brute); a scrap of
  ground shadow can stay at the feet (crane 107, wizard 104).
- flatten.py v3 (hysteresis Ether mask, small vivid features kept, wider lightning
  window, same-hue tone merging): the shadow scraps are gone, and the lion's and the
  wizard's red eyes hold. Not fixed: the crane's robe still gets cyan stripes. New
  problem: the wider lightning window takes tan skin as Ether (the gnome's face and
  hands turn orange; boar 106's snout becomes a pale yellow smear). The antler
  horror's zigzag spark is still lost, a non-Ether eye colour (the wizard's yellow
  eyes) still drops out, and the brute is only slightly less blotchy.
- flatten.py v4 (Ether must glow and stand out from its surroundings; base-and-shade
  body tones), run by the Mac on the 43 iteration 5 picks: the best yet. The crane's
  robe stripes, the gnome's orange face and the boar's snout smear are gone; the
  antler horror's zigzag and the wizard's eyes are kept; the brute's blotches are
  much reduced. Its strictness now drops big, pale, soft glows: crane 101's pale blue
  sword glow disappears entirely, crane 108's becomes a thin white crackle, gath-brute
  101's blue crackle and goblin-hexer 106's pale smoke are gone, and twin-ogre 104's
  cyan flame turns into a dark squiggle (kept as body, then outlined). Pale Ether in
  the raw image is the risky case.
- flatten.py v5 (a soft-glow path for water and shadow; keep_ink keeps the drawing's
  dark lines): crane 101's halo, crane 108's glow, the twin ogre's flame (cyan, not
  dark) and the brute's and goblin's glows are back; crane 107 and the gnome stay
  clean. Big glows come out as one solid flat shape, which suits the flat look. The
  hexer's pale smoke is still faint.
- Iteration 6: all 16 enemies x 8 seeds (109-116), the iteration 5 prompts with the
  vector opening of STYLE, no references. Raw images on the share under i6\raw\.
- The approach is volume: many seeds per creature, then pick, rather than perfecting
  the prompt for every image.

STYLE (appended to every prompt; changed in iterations 4 and 5, see the lessons)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A flat vector illustration: minimalistic graphic design in flat colour with clean
lines, made of big, simple, readable shapes with smooth clean edges. Every form is
one flat, matte base colour, with at most a single hard-edged darker shade tone along
its lower edge. Large plain areas of colour with little surface detail; fur, hair and
cloth are smooth solid shapes. A limited, muted, earthy palette, with the creature's
Ether energy as the only bright, saturated colour. The creature is shown whole and
alone, centred, in side view, filling about four fifths of the picture's height, its
feet at the bottom margin, on a plain, evenly lit, flat light grey background that is
empty all around and beneath it.
Its head and limbs are clearly separate from its body. The creature is ferocious and
dangerous, a savage monster rather than a cute character: a heavy, scowling brow over
narrow, fierce eyes with no visible pupils, a snarl that bares its fangs, and a
powerful, battle-hardened build.


EMANATION (follows every subject; fill in the blanks from the tables)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Its eyes glow bright [COLOUR]. Bright [COLOUR] Ether energy emanates from its [PART],
[TEXTURE], as if its power is leaking out of it.

  Ether      COLOUR          TEXTURE
  fire       ember-red       rising off them in flickering tongues of flame
  water      icy blue        rising off them in curling wisps of frost and mist
  lightning  amber-orange    crackling off them in jagged sparks and arcs
  shadow     violet          rising off them in curling wisps of smoke

[PART] is one place, picked on purpose for each creature: its mouth, its hands or its
weapon. The energy appears only there and in the eyes.
